THE OBSERVATORY

A Gateway Between Earth and the Cosmos

Perched at 3,650 metres in the heart of the Himalayas, the observatory was built for a single purpose — to place you face to face with the night sky.

Its instruments and astronomers do more than observe. They translate the science of stars, planets and nebulae into experiences you can see with your own eyes.

THE SETTING

Why Look to the Sky From Here?

At this altitude, above most of the atmosphere's turbulence and all of the valley's light, the Himalayan sky behaves differently. Stars hold steady. The Milky Way shows its full band.

HIGH ALTITUDE
Thinner air means steadier seeing — a clearer atmosphere between you and the stars.
DARK SKY
A Bortle-class-2 sky — among the darkest skies reachable without an expedition.
LOW LIGHT POLLUTION
The nearest settlements sit in deep valleys, their light screened by the ridgelines.
CLEAR HORIZONS
Open alpine plateaus give an unobstructed view from horizon to horizon.
